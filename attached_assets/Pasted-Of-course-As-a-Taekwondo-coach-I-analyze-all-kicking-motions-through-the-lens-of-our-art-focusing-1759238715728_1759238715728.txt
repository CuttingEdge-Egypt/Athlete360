### **Overall Assessment**

In Taekwondo, a kick's power doesn't come from the leg alone; it originates from the ground, travels through the hips and core, and is released through the leg like the tip of a whip. I will be evaluating each person based on these core principles: **Stance (Pivoting Foot), Hip Rotation, Chamber/Execution, Upper Body Control (Guard), and Follow-through.**

### **Analysis of Shooter #1: Leighton (Light blue/grey shirt)**

**Timestamp:** 0:04 - 0:19

**Strengths:** * **Decent Set-Up:** He establishes a stable base before initiating his kick, planting his non-kicking foot reasonably well. This is fundamental for any technique. * **Good Intent:** He clearly understands he needs to generate power and attempts to put force behind his kicks.

**Areas for Improvement:** * **Lack of Hip Rotation:** This is his biggest technical flaw. His hips remain almost completely square to the target throughout the kick. He is "arming" the kick with his leg, resulting in a disconnected push rather than a powerful, snapping strike. In Taekwondo, this would result in a very weak board break or a push-kick in sparring. * **Poor Upper Body Control:** His arms are down and flail for balance. A proper kick requires the upper body to be engaged. The opposite arm should be tight in a guard position to maintain balance, protect the body, and add counter-torque for power. * **No Chambering:** The kick is a straight-leg swing. A proper Taekwondo kick involves "chambering" the knee (bringing it up and bent) before snapping the lower leg out to the target. This chambering action dramatically increases speed and power.

**Actionable Steps & Drills:** 1. **Hip Rotation Drill:** Stand sideways to a wall or heavy bag. Place your kicking foot forward. Without swinging your leg much, practice rotating your hips over as if you were kicking. Focus on pivoting your supporting foot until your heel points towards the target. Do this 20 times on each side to build muscle memory. 2. **Chamber Snap Drill:** Hold onto a chair for balance. Practice chambering your knee high and tight to your chest, then snap your lower leg out and immediately retract it back to the chamber position. This isolates the "snapping" motion. 3. **Guard Hand Discipline:** Practice all kicking drills in front of a mirror, ensuring your non-kicking hand stays glued to your jawline.
